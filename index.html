About

From polarized line formation to the magnetic corona

My research trajectory spans fundamental polarized-line formation, scattering polarization and partial frequency redistribution, through to multi-dimensional coronal diagnostics and magnetic-field inference. At the centre of this work is P-CORONA, an open-source framework I developed for synthesizing the full Stokes profiles of permitted and forbidden coronal lines in realistic three-dimensional atmospheres.

I currently extend this programme through cross-code benchmarking, Ne VIII Doppler-dimming studies, and non-Maxwellian line-formation calculations. My broader goal is to move coronal magnetometry from detecting magnetic signatures toward determining which physical quantities can be recovered reliably from spectropolarimetric observations.

Research

Research themes

Coronal magnetometry

Quantifying how Hanle- and Zeeman-sensitive coronal lines encode magnetic-field information, and how line-of-sight integration limits what can be inferred.

P-CORONA

Developing and applying a unified full-Stokes forward-modelling framework for permitted and forbidden coronal lines in arbitrary 3D atmospheres.

Polarized radiative transfer

Modelling scattering polarization, redistribution physics, anisotropic illumination, Doppler dimming, atomic polarization, and magnetic-field effects in solar spectral lines.

Current direction

CORAL

CORAL develops a recoverability-first approach to coronal spectropolarimetry: determine what information survives line-of-sight integration, identify which diagnostic combinations break specific degeneracies, and preserve ambiguity when the data do not support a unique solution.

Lead developer and principal implementer of an open-source coronal spectropolarimetric modelling framework, released with documentation, reference examples, and reproducibility inputs.

2025

P-CORONA: A New Tool for Calculating the Intensity and Polarization of Coronal Lines in 3D Models of the Solar Corona

Lead-author study establishing a unified route from a realistic 3D coronal state to observable full-Stokes signatures.

2025

Reduced Atomic Models for Large-Scale Computations: Fe XIII Near-Infrared Lines

Applied reduced atomic models to Fe XIII polarization, demonstrating substantially lower computational cost while retaining the relevant polarization physics.

Theoretical study of permitted-line polarization and the influence of Doppler dimming and anisotropic illumination caused by solar-wind velocities.

Science communication & widening participation
